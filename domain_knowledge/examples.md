# Few-Shot Examples

## Example 1: The Ant and the Grasshopper
- Genre: fable
- Theme: responsibility and preparation
- Audience: children
- Reading level: easy
- Moral: Plan ahead and work steadily before difficult times arrive.

## Example 2: The Proud Rose
- Genre: moral tale
- Theme: pride, change, and humility
- Audience: general
- Reading level: easy
- Moral: Pride can isolate us, while humility helps us grow.

## Example 3: The Starfish Thrower
- Genre: short story
- Theme: compassion and small acts of kindness
- Audience: general
- Reading level: easy
- Moral: Even small acts of help matter when they improve one life at a time.

## Example 4: A Story About Waiting
- Genre: moral tale
- Theme: patience and self-control
- Audience: children
- Reading level: easy
- Moral: Waiting calmly can lead to better outcomes than acting in haste.

## Example 5: The Lost Path
- Genre: adventure
- Theme: courage, guidance, and persistence
- Audience: middle grade
- Reading level: middle
- Moral: Staying determined and asking for help can turn confusion into progress.

## Example 6: The Quiet Town
- Genre: realism
- Theme: community, change, and responsibility
- Audience: general
- Reading level: middle
- Moral: Small choices by ordinary people can shape an entire community.

## Classification Principle
Use the same category labels and similar wording when the evidence is the same. Prefer stable outputs over creative variation.

## Edge-Case Principle
- If a story looks like both a fable and a moral tale, choose fable when animal or symbolic characters drive the lesson.
- If a story is grounded in ordinary life, choose realism even if it has a strong lesson.
- If the text is too short to prove a complex genre, use the broadest correct label and say why.
