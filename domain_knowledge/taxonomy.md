# Librarian Taxonomy

## Core Classification Fields
- Genre: fable, short story, folk tale, adventure, moral tale, realism, fantasy, parable, myth, legend, or other.
- Theme: the main idea or recurring topic, such as kindness, perseverance, honesty, sacrifice, friendship, grief, redemption, responsibility, or learning from mistakes.
- Audience: children, middle grade, young adult, or general.
- Reading level: easy, middle, or advanced.
- Tone: playful, reflective, serious, hopeful, cautionary, or inspirational.
- Structure: episodic, linear, dialogue-driven, allegorical, or reflective.

## Classification Rules
- Prefer stable, simple categories over overly specific labels.
- Use the dominant story purpose, not a single scene, to choose the category.
- If a story has an explicit moral, treat it as a moral tale or fable when appropriate.
- If the evidence is weak, use conservative labels such as short story and general.
- Keep the same category choices across repeated runs when the text has not changed.
- If a story centers on a lesson or consequence, prioritize moral tale or fable before broader genres.
- If the story is driven by symbolic meaning more than plot mechanics, consider parable or allegory.
- If the text is brief but emotionally focused, classify by theme and tone instead of forcing a complex genre label.
- Do not change labels just to sound more specific; consistency is more valuable than creativity.

## Genre Hints
- Fable: short story with animal or symbolic characters and a clear lesson.
- Moral tale: story built around a lesson, ethical choice, or consequence.
- Parable: concise story that teaches a deeper truth through analogy.
- Folk tale: traditional story passed through oral-style patterns.
- Adventure: plot emphasizes journey, challenge, or action.
- Fantasy: includes magical, impossible, or clearly unreal elements.
- Realism: grounded in ordinary life and believable situations.

## Rationale Standard
When explaining a category choice, mention:
- the dominant theme,
- the main audience,
- and the textual evidence that supports the decision.
- If a category is uncertain, mention the uncertainty and choose the most conservative label.

## Stability Rule
When two books are similar, keep the same label vocabulary, the same reading-level scale, and the same explanation style.
